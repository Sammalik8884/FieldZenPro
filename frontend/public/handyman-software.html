Flat-Rate Pricing for Handyman Services

The shift from hourly billing to flat-rate pricing is the single highest-impact operational change most handyman businesses make when they professionalise their operations. Hourly billing creates three problems: it rewards slow work (a slower technician earns more per job, creating a perverse incentive), it requires the customer to accept an open-ended cost commitment at booking (which creates anxiety that depresses job acceptance rates), and it consistently underprices jobs where the technician's expertise allows them to complete the work faster than an average rate would suggest. A 30-minute door hinge replacement billed at $85/hour earns $42.50. The same job billed at a flat rate of $95 earns $95 — in half the time, with no customer uncertainty about the final bill and no technician incentive to work slowly.

Each task is priced at the market rate for your service area. The price book is fully editable — you set the prices for your market. When a technician arrives at a job, they open the price book in the mobile app, select all tasks to be completed during the visit, and the invoice total populates automatically. The customer approves the total before the work starts or signs off at completion — no pricing surprise, no negotiation, no dispute.

Multi-Trade Job Quoting: Eliminating the Scope Creep Dispute Before It Starts

Handyman businesses lose more money to scope creep disputes than almost any other service trade — because handyman work, almost by definition, involves a customer who started the conversation asking for one thing, discovered two more things while the technician was there, and now has a different mental model of what the invoice should reflect than the technician who did the work. The customer who said "just fix the door and replace the bathroom faucet" remembers adding the light switch replacement as a casual request, not as a billable addition. If the invoice shows three line items and the customer only remembers authorising two jobs, the dispute is guaranteed. The handyman who cannot produce a signed scope document for every item loses that dispute every time.

FieldZenPro's on-site quote workflow handles multi-trade jobs through a line-item approval process on the mobile app. When the technician arrives and the customer shows them an additional item — the stuck drawer, the dripping outdoor faucet, the loose handrail — the technician adds it to the job ticket in real time as a separate quoted line item. The customer reviews the full list on the technician's device and approves the complete scope digitally before any additional work begins. The approved quote becomes the invoice — every line item the customer signed off on is billed; nothing appears on the invoice that was not pre-approved. When the invoice arrives, the customer compares it to the digital approval they received as a confirmation email — and the amounts match. Disputes from scope ambiguity drop to zero. Technicians stop writing off add-on work to keep customers happy. Revenue per job increases because technicians quote every legitimate additional item instead of avoiding the conversation.

Photo Documentation: The Handyman's Liability Shield on Every Job

Handyman work carries a specific liability exposure that differs from plumbing or electrical — because handyman technicians work across multiple unrelated trades in a single visit, the causal connection between their work and any subsequent problem is always ambiguous from the customer's perspective. When the bathroom faucet that was not touched starts dripping two weeks after a handyman visit that included a sink replacement in the same bathroom, the customer's first assumption is that the handyman caused the problem. If the handyman has no documentation of the pre-existing condition of the dripping faucet from the job visit, the dispute is their word against the customer's — and the customer's narrative of causation will prevail.

FieldZenPro's photo documentation workflow requires technicians to photograph every area of work at three points: before work begins (pre-condition documentation), during work at key stages (progress documentation), and after work is complete (completion documentation). Photos are automatically tagged with the job number, technician ID, GPS location, and timestamp — creating an unalterable record that cannot be backdated. Pre-condition photos document every pre-existing defect in the work area: the already-dripping faucet, the existing wall damage near the TV mount installation, the cracked tile adjacent to the grout repair. When a customer claims the handyman caused a pre-existing problem, the pre-condition photo from that job visit — timestamped, geotagged, stored permanently in the job record — closes the dispute immediately. The handyman business that documents every job this systematically stops absorbing the cost of work they did not cause.
