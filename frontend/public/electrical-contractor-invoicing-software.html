Progress Billing for Commercial Electrical Projects

Progress billing is the standard invoicing method for commercial electrical installations — from a $50,000 office build-out electrical package to a $2M industrial facility wiring project. The contractor and the general contractor or owner agree on a schedule of values at project start: a breakdown of the total contract amount across defined phases of work, each assigned a dollar value proportional to its estimated cost and scope. As each phase is completed, the contractor submits a progress billing application — an invoice for the phase value less the agreed retainage percentage. The general contractor reviews the application, potentially disputes items, and releases payment for the approved amount within the contract's payment terms.

FieldZenPro's progress billing module structures each commercial electrical project as a schedule of values at project creation. Each schedule item has a description, a percentage of the total contract value, and a dollar amount calculated from the total contract price. As the project progresses and phases are completed, the project manager marks each schedule item complete in FieldZenPro. The system immediately calculates the billing amount for the completed items — contract value of completed items less retainage percentage — and generates the billing application invoice. The invoice is formatted as a standard AIA G702/G703 billing application structure, showing the schedule of values, previously billed amounts, current billing amount, retainage withheld, and balance to complete for each line item.

Change orders are managed within the project record in FieldZenPro. When the scope of a commercial electrical project changes — additional circuits required by revised mechanical drawings, panel upgrades required by increased load calculations, additional emergency lighting circuits required by the fire marshal — the change order is created in the project with its approved dollar value and any schedule impact. Approved change orders update the project's schedule of values automatically, adding the change order amount to the total contract value and to the appropriate phase of work. The updated schedule of values flows to the next billing application, ensuring all approved scope additions are invoiced correctly without any manual recalculation.

Retainage Tracking: Automatic Deduction and QuickBooks Accounting

Retainage — the percentage of each progress invoice withheld by the owner or general contractor until project completion and acceptance — is the most financially significant tracking requirement in commercial electrical invoicing. A 10% retainage on a $500,000 electrical project means $50,000 is withheld across the project's billing applications and released only when the project is accepted and any punchlist items are resolved. Managing retainage manually — calculating the deduction on each invoice, tracking the cumulative withheld amount, and generating the final retainage release invoice — is a time-consuming and error-prone process when done on a spreadsheet.

FieldZenPro calculates and tracks retainage automatically. The retainage percentage is configured at project creation (commonly 10%, sometimes reducing to 5% after 50% project completion per the contract terms). Every progress billing invoice deducts the configured retainage percentage from the earned amount automatically — the contractor does not need to calculate the deduction for each invoice. The project dashboard shows the cumulative retainage withheld across all billing applications at all times. When the project is accepted and the owner authorises retainage release, FieldZenPro generates the retainage release invoice for the total accumulated retainage balance from all billing applications — a single accurate number with a clear audit trail of the billing applications that contributed to it.

Retainage syncs to QuickBooks with correct revenue recognition accounting. The withheld retainage is recorded as a receivable contingent on project completion — it does not appear as earned revenue until the retainage release invoice is paid. This is the technically correct accounting treatment under GAAP and is required for accurate project profitability reporting. Electrical contractors who previously recorded retainage as earned revenue at billing time were inflating their revenue figures and understating their uncollected receivables — FieldZenPro's retainage accounting corrects this automatically.

T&M Invoicing for Service Calls: GPS Hours, Not Memory

Time-and-material invoicing for electrical service calls has one critical vulnerability in paper-based systems: the accuracy of the labour hours recorded. Electricians on a busy service day, running 4–6 calls, are not tracking their time to the minute on each job. They estimate. They round. And the rounding is almost always downward — a job that actually took 2 hours and 40 minutes becomes "about 2.5 hours" on the paper job card. Across a 10-electrician team doing 40 service calls per day, that downward rounding accumulates into significant unbilled labour revenue — conservatively 15–20 minutes per job on average, at $90–$125 per hour, across 200 working days per year.

FieldZenPro's GPS geofence billing eliminates estimation entirely. When an electrician enters the geofenced radius around the job site address, the system records the arrival timestamp. When they depart, the departure timestamp is recorded. The difference is the on-site duration, applied at the configured labour rate for that electrician's level. Not "about 2.5 hours" — 2 hours and 42 minutes at the actual on-site rate, calculated automatically, with the GPS timestamps as the supporting record for any customer query about the billed time. The GPS billing record is objective, timestamped, and accurate to the minute — the strongest possible evidence for the billed labour amount in any payment dispute.

Flat-Rate Invoicing for Standard Electrical Work

Not all residential electrical work is appropriate for T&M billing. Standard, well-defined electrical tasks — outlet replacement, light switch installation, light fixture hanging, GFCI outlet installation, AFCI circuit breaker replacement, smoke detector installation, EV charger outlet installation, ceiling fan installation — have predictable scope and predictable completion times for an experienced electrician. For these tasks, flat-rate invoicing gives the customer certainty about the cost, removes the customer's natural anxiety about watching an hourly worker and calculating the running total, and allows the electrical business to capture above-average margin on tasks completed efficiently by experienced electricians.

FieldZenPro's flat-rate price book for electrical work is configured by the business owner with prices for each standard electrical service type. The price book is organised by category — residential outlet work, residential panel work, residential lighting, EV and generator, commercial lighting, commercial panel work — and is searchable on the electrician's mobile app. During a service call, the electrician selects all applicable flat-rate line items for the work performed. The invoice generates from the selected items at the configured prices. Since all electricians use the same price book, there is no pricing variation between technicians on identical work types — a GFCI outlet installation costs $X regardless of which electrician performs it, ensuring consistent customer experience and consistent gross margin across the team.
