Licence Enforcement: Gas, Backflow, and Medical Gas Requirements

Plumbing licensing requirements vary more by jurisdiction and job type than most trade contractors appreciate until they face a compliance inspection. A journeyman plumber in many states cannot legally perform new installation work without a master plumber on the permit. Gas line work requires a separate gas fitter licence in most states — a plumber without a gas fitter endorsement who performs gas line repairs is creating legal exposure for the business regardless of how competent they are as a plumber. Backflow prevention device testing and certification requires an ASSE-approved backflow tester certification. Medical gas piping in healthcare facilities requires ASSE 6010 brazing qualification and medical gas certification — not simply a plumbing licence.

FieldZenPro's licence enforcement system stores each plumber's complete qualification profile: plumber licence level (apprentice, journeyman, master, licensed contractor), gas fitter endorsement and issuing state, backflow tester certification and certification body, ASSE 6010 medical gas certification, cross-connection control specialist certification, and any other trade-specific qualifications relevant to the business's service portfolio. Each qualification has an expiry date tracked by the system, with automated alerts at 60 and 30 days before expiry.

Job types are configured with their required qualifications — gas line repair requires gas fitter licence, backflow testing requires backflow certification, new installation requiring permit requires master plumber oversight, medical gas work requires ASSE 6010. When the dispatcher creates a job of a configured type, the assignment panel shows only plumbers whose current qualification profiles include all required credentials for that job type. A plumber with a journeyman licence but no gas fitter endorsement simply does not appear as an assignment option for gas line jobs. The compliance enforcement happens at the point of dispatch, automatically, without the dispatcher needing to check each plumber's individual credential file before every assignment.

Parts Inventory Pre-Dispatch: Reducing Return Trips

Return trips are one of the most damaging operational inefficiencies in plumbing service — a plumber arrives at a job, diagnoses the problem, and then has to return to the shop or drive to a supplier because the required part is not in their van. The job is delayed. The customer's day is disrupted a second time. The plumber has burned 30–60 minutes of billable time on unproductive transit. And the job that was supposed to follow in the afternoon schedule is pushed back, cascading into appointment window violations for later customers.

FieldZenPro's van stock management system tracks the current inventory of each plumber's van — a list of standard repair parts maintained in each plumber's profile, updated when parts are used on jobs (logged from the digital work order materials section) and when stock is restocked from the shop. When a new job is dispatched, the system compares the job type's typical parts requirements against the dispatched plumber's current van stock. If a commonly needed part for the job type — a specific pressure relief valve, a cartridge for the customer's faucet brand, a wax ring in the appropriate size — is not in the plumber's van stock, the dispatcher sees a parts alert before the plumber departs.

The parts alert allows the dispatcher to either route the plumber via the shop to collect the required parts, arrange for a parts delivery to the job site from a nearby supplier, or reassign the job to a plumber whose van stock includes the needed components. The parts alert does not automatically delay the dispatch — for emergency jobs, the dispatcher may accept the risk of a missing part and deal with it if the diagnosis confirms the part is needed. For scheduled non-emergency jobs, the parts pre-check ensures the plumber arrives with everything needed to complete the job in one visit. Plumbing businesses implementing van stock management in FieldZenPro consistently report reducing their return trip rate from 30–40% of jobs to below 12% within 90 days — a significant improvement in job completion rate, plumber productivity, and customer satisfaction.

Water Damage Urgency Triage: Prioritising the Right Jobs

Not all plumbing calls are equally urgent, and treating them as if they are wastes emergency response capacity on low-urgency work while high-urgency situations wait in the same queue. Urgency triage gives the dispatcher a structured framework for classifying incoming jobs and routing them through the appropriate dispatch workflow rather than handling them all first-come-first-served regardless of severity.

FieldZenPro's triage classification system uses four severity levels for plumbing dispatch. Level 1 — Active Emergency: water is currently flowing, leak is active, property damage is ongoing. Dispatch target: plumber on site within 60 minutes. Emergency dispatch workflow activated. Level 2 — High Risk: water has been stopped (shutoff valve closed by customer) but structural damage, mould risk, or service disruption makes rapid resolution important. Dispatch target: same day, within 4 hours. Priority queue position. Level 3 — Scheduled Service: non-urgent repair, maintenance visit, or installation with an agreed appointment date and time. Standard scheduling workflow. Level 4 — After Hours Emergency: emergency received outside business hours requiring on-call plumber with emergency rate billing. On-call dispatch workflow activated.

The triage level is set by the dispatcher when the job is logged, based on the customer's description during the intake call. Jobs display on the scheduling board with their triage level visible — Level 1 jobs appear in red, Level 2 in amber, Level 3 in standard colour, Level 4 with an after-hours indicator. The dispatcher sees at a glance which jobs need immediate attention and which are appropriately queued. When a new emergency arrives while the dispatcher is managing a busy board, the triage classification ensures it surfaces at the top of the queue automatically rather than sitting invisibly behind a stack of routine jobs.
