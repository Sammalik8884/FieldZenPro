Certification and Licence Tracking

Every field service trade involves professional credentials that determine what a technician is legally and professionally qualified to perform. Electrical work requires state-issued electrician licences at the appropriate level — apprentice, journeyman, master. Plumbing requires plumbing licences with endorsements for gas fitting and backflow testing. HVAC requires EPA 608 certification for refrigerant handling and may require a state HVAC contractor licence for the business and a technician licence for the individual. Security system installation requires state alarm installer licences in most jurisdictions. These credentials are not optional paperwork — they are legal requirements whose violation creates regulatory risk for the business and compliance exposure for the customer's insurance coverage.

FieldZenPro's certification management stores each credential per technician: the credential type, the issuing authority (state licensing board, EPA, NATE, manufacturer), the licence or certification number, and the expiry date. For credentials with no expiry date (some manufacturer training certifications), the issue date is stored instead. Two automated workflows fire from the credential database: approaching expiry alerts notify the operations manager when a technician's credential is within 60 days of expiry, creating time to coordinate renewal before the expiry date arrives; and expired credential removal removes the technician from the dispatch pool for job types requiring that credential, effective immediately on the expiry date, without any manual action from the dispatcher. The business's compliance posture is maintained continuously, without depending on any individual's memory of which credentials expire when.

Skills-Based Dispatch: The Right Technician Every Time

Skills-based dispatch is the operational implementation of the credential management system — translating the stored credentials into dispatch filters that present only qualified technicians for each job type. In FieldZenPro, each job type is configured with a set of credential requirements. When a dispatcher creates a job and assigns it to a technician, the available technician list is automatically filtered to show only those who meet all credential requirements for that job type.

The practical effect of skills-based dispatch is twofold. First, compliance is enforced structurally rather than procedurally — the dispatcher does not need to know which technicians are qualified for gas line work; the system shows only gas-fitting-licensed technicians for gas line jobs. Second, job quality is improved — the right technician means not just the legally qualified technician, but the most experienced and effective technician for the specific job type. NATE-certified VRF system technicians are assigned to VRF jobs. Experienced commercial electricians are assigned to commercial panel work. Apprentices are assigned to simpler work within their qualification scope and availability, building experience without exposure to jobs beyond their current qualification level.

Skills-based dispatch also serves as a training and career development tool — the operations manager can see which technicians have the broadest qualification profiles (eligible for the widest range of job types) and which have narrow profiles that limit their dispatch eligibility. Technicians with narrow profiles can be prioritised for additional training and certification that expands their deployment options and, in many businesses, triggers a pay grade advancement.

Performance Analytics: Managing with Data

FieldZenPro's technician performance report provides a complete, multi-dimensional view of each technician's output for any selected time period — daily, weekly, monthly, or custom date range. The five performance dimensions tracked per technician are: jobs completed (volume productivity), revenue generated (financial productivity), revenue per job (average job value), first-time fix rate (quality), and customer satisfaction score (customer experience). Each dimension is reported as an absolute value and as a comparison against the team average, making performance variation immediately visible without requiring the manager to calculate benchmarks manually.

The performance report enables three specific management interventions that were previously impossible without platform data. First, early identification of declining performance: a technician whose first-time fix rate has dropped from 88% to 71% over a 30-day period is showing a performance signal that, left unaddressed, will produce customer complaints and costly repeat visits. The manager who sees this trend in real time can investigate and intervene — is it a parts inventory issue, a training gap, a personal situation affecting focus? — before the trend produces compounding damage. Second, recognition of high performance: a technician generating 35% more revenue per day than the team average is a high performer whose practices are worth understanding and sharing with the team. Third, objective data for pay decisions: performance reviews and pay adjustments based on documented platform data — revenue generated, jobs completed, customer satisfaction scores — are more motivating and more defensible than decisions based on manager impression alone.

Technician Onboarding and Subcontractor Management

Getting a new technician operational in FieldZenPro takes 10–15 minutes. The operations manager creates the technician profile, enters their credentials — trade licence, specialty endorsements, EPA certifications — with expiry dates, assigns their service zones and job type eligibility based on their qualification profile, installs the mobile app on their phone, and creates their login credentials. The new technician is immediately visible on the scheduling board, GPS-trackable on the live map, eligible for dispatch to qualified job types, and fully functional on the mobile app — operational on day one, with no IT support or technical implementation required.

Subcontractor management follows the same profile structure. Subcontractor technicians are stored with their own credential profile, assigned service zones, and job type eligibility — but with a separate billing rate configuration reflecting the subcontractor's cost to the business rather than an employee's loaded hourly cost. Jobs assigned to subcontractors generate the same work orders, GPS billing records, and photo documentation as employee technician jobs — giving the business the same quality assurance and billing accuracy for subcontracted work as for directly employed technicians. Insurance and liability certificate details for each subcontractor are stored in their profile, with expiry alerts ensuring that subcontractors with lapsed insurance are flagged before they are assigned to new jobs.

The combined employee and subcontractor workforce is visible in a single unified scheduling board and GPS map — the dispatcher sees all available resources regardless of employment type and can make optimal assignment decisions from the complete pool of qualified technicians. The operations manager's performance analytics distinguish between employee and subcontractor output, enabling comparison of direct employment cost efficiency versus subcontractor cost for each service category — a data-driven foundation for workforce mix decisions as the business grows.
