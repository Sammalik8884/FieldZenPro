Roofing is a high-value, project-based field service business with a documentation intensity that exceeds most other trades — particularly for the insurance restoration segment, which constitutes 40–60% of residential roofing revenue in hail-active markets. An insurance replacement job requires the roofing contractor to document the pre-work damage condition with photos, capture the adjuster's scope of work and approved amounts, invoice against the insurance company's payment schedule (ACV payment, depreciation release, supplements), track the materials installed for manufacturer warranty registration, document the completed installation with photos, and generate a workmanship warranty certificate — all for a single job. Managing this documentation workflow on paper or in generic spreadsheets leads to missed supplements, delayed depreciation release payments, voided manufacturer warranties due to incomplete registration, and warranty claim disputes due to inadequate installation documentation.

Beyond insurance restoration, roofing contractors managing retail replacement and commercial roofing projects face project billing complexity — progress invoicing at material delivery and completion milestones, retainage tracking on commercial projects, change order documentation when additional work is identified during tear-off, and subcontractor coordination for labour-only installation crews. The combination of insurance documentation complexity, project billing complexity, and subcontractor management makes roofing one of the most software-intensive trades in the construction and specialty contracting sector — and one where the right operational platform has a disproportionate impact on profitability and cash flow.

Insurance Claim Roofing: Documentation, Billing & Supplement Management

The insurance claim roofing workflow is the most documentation-intensive job type in the residential roofing sector. From the initial inspection through the final depreciation release payment, an insurance claim job involves capturing and managing more distinct data points than any other roofing job type — and the accuracy and completeness of that documentation directly determines the total revenue the contractor collects from each job. A supplement for additional decking damage discovered during tear-off can add $800–$2,500 to the job value, but only if the damage is documented with photos before the new decking is installed and the supplement request is submitted to the adjuster with supporting evidence. A supplement for ice and water shield upgraded from standard to full-deck coverage adds $600–$1,200, but requires the adjuster's approval before the additional billing can be collected. These supplements are the difference between an insurance job that delivers target margin and one that delivers break-even margin — and they are almost entirely a documentation and claim management issue, not a labour or materials issue.

FieldZenPro's insurance claim workflow captures the adjuster scope line by line from the initial adjuster report, tracks the approved amounts by category (shingles, underlayment, ice and water shield, decking, flashing, vents, gutters, labour), and records the ACV payment, the depreciation withhold, and the deductible. As work progresses and additional damage is documented, supplement requests are created from the job record with supporting photos attached — the supplement documentation package is generated as a formatted PDF ready for adjuster submission. Approved supplements are added to the job's approved amount. The final invoice reflects the complete approved scope, and the depreciation release invoice is generated when the completion documentation is uploaded — triggering the insurance company's release of the recoverable depreciation payment to the homeowner and contractor.

Material Tracking and Waste Factor Management

Material cost is the largest single cost component of a roofing job — typically 40–55% of the retail contract price on a residential replacement. Accurate material tracking per job is essential for understanding actual job profitability versus estimated profitability, for identifying waste factor issues that are inflating material cost above estimate, and for maintaining the manufacturer warranty registration records that protect the contractor and the customer in the event of a product defect warranty claim. FieldZenPro's material tracking records all materials ordered and delivered per job — shingle manufacturer, style, colour, and quantity (squares); underlayment type and roll count; ice and water shield lineal footage; ridge cap bundle count; starter strip bundle count; nails and fasteners; pipe boots and vents; and all other materials. The material delivery is confirmed against the supplier purchase order, and any variance (short delivery, substitution) is flagged for resolution before the installation crew starts work.

Waste factor analysis compares the squares of shingles ordered versus the squares of roof surface installed — the difference is the waste percentage. Industry standard waste factors range from 10–15% for a simple gable roof to 20–25% for a complex hip or intersecting valley roof. Roofing contractors who consistently order at the correct waste factor for each roof complexity level optimise their material cost and eliminate both the margin cost of over-ordering and the delay cost of running short and waiting for a second delivery. FieldZenPro's job profitability report tracks waste factor per job and per crew over time — identifying crews who consistently run high waste factors, which may indicate cutting technique issues, material handling waste, or incorrect ordering practices that require training or supervision attention.
