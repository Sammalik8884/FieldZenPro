Why Electrical Dispatch Requires License-Level Intelligence

Electrical dispatch is not just scheduling -- it is credential management under regulatory threat. Every jurisdiction in the United States defines specific tasks that only licensed master electricians may perform or supervise, tasks that journeymen may execute independently, and tasks that apprentices may assist with under the supervision of a journeyman or master at a defined ratio. Sending an apprentice to pull a permit in a jurisdiction that requires a master license creates a code violation. Dispatching a journeyman to a service panel replacement job in a jurisdiction that requires a master sign-off on the permit creates liability for your company and your qualifier.

Generic field service software has no concept of electrical license levels, jurisdiction-specific permit authority, or apprentice supervision ratios. FieldZenPro was designed with this structure built in. Every electrician in your team carries their license level, jurisdiction authorizations, and permit-pull rights in their profile. Every job carries its jurisdiction, minimum credential requirement, and permit requirement flag. When a dispatcher creates a job, the system ensures that only qualified technicians with the correct license level for that job in that jurisdiction can be assigned.

License-Level Dispatch Filtering: Apprentice, Journeyman, Master

Each electrician in FieldZenPro carries a credential profile: license level (apprentice, journeyman, master), license number, issuing state, expiry date, and jurisdiction-specific permit authority. Jobs carry a minimum credential requirement configured by job type or set manually per job. When a dispatcher opens the assignment picker, only electricians meeting the credential requirement for that job in that jurisdiction appear as available options.

The system enforces credential-based dispatch at the data layer, not through training or hope.

Permit Tracking by Jurisdiction and Job Type

Electrical permits are job-critical documents. A rough-in inspection cannot happen without an open permit. Final inspection cannot clear without the permit being finalized. Permits expire if inspections are not completed within the jurisdiction's timeframe -- often 180 days. For electrical contractors running 50 or more active jobs simultaneously, tracking which jobs have open permits, which permits need inspections scheduled, and which are approaching expiration requires systematic management that spreadsheets cannot provide.

FieldZenPro tracks permit number, permit type (electrical, low voltage, temporary service), jurisdiction, issue date, expiry date, rough-in inspection status, and final inspection status per job. Permit expiration alerts fire 60 and 30 days before expiry. When a rough-in inspection is passed, the system prompts creation of a final inspection scheduling task. The permit record links to the job's inspection photos and correction notice documentation.

Permit Number and Status Tracking

Electrical emergencies -- a tripped main breaker in a restaurant, a failed service panel in an apartment building, an arc fault in a commercial kitchen -- require immediate response. These are master electrician jobs that cannot be deferred. FieldZenPro's emergency dispatch system flags panel emergency jobs at the highest priority level. After-hours emergency calls route to the on-call master electrician automatically. Journeyman-to-Apprentice Ratio Compliance

Most states define a maximum number of apprentices that a single journeyman or master electrician may supervise on a job site simultaneously -- commonly one or two apprentices per journeyman. This ratio requirement is part of electrical licensing compliance and can affect your contractor license if violated. When you have multiple crews across multiple job sites, maintaining ratio compliance manually is error-prone. A journeyman calls in sick, an apprentice gets redirected -- suddenly a job site has two unsupervised apprentices without anyone noticing.

FieldZenPro tracks the journeyman-to-apprentice ratio per active job site in real time. When an apprentice is dispatched to a job, the system checks whether a journeyman or master is assigned to the same job within the required ratio. If the ratio would be violated by the assignment -- for example, a second apprentice being dispatched to a job where the journeyman already has one apprentice and no master is on site -- the system flags the assignment with a ratio warning before confirming dispatch.

⚖

Real-Time Ratio Monitoring Per Job Site

Live dashboard shows journeyman-to-apprentice ratio for every active job site. Sites approaching or exceeding the maximum ratio are flagged. Project managers see ratio status across all jobs simultaneously without having to open individual job records.

🔔

Dispatch Ratio Warning System

When assigning an apprentice to a job, FieldZenPro checks the resulting ratio against your configured state requirement. Assignments that would create a ratio violation display a warning before confirming. The dispatcher must acknowledge the warning and can escalate to manager approval before proceeding.
