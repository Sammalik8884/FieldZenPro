Field service businesses lose money in their billing processes in three specific ways, and most business owners cannot see any of them clearly until they have a system that makes the losses visible. The first loss is unbilled work — services performed and materials used that never make it onto an invoice because the field data collection process (paper job cards, verbal notes, memory) is too unreliable. Industry benchmarking consistently shows that field service businesses using paper-based billing capture 75–88% of their billable materials — meaning 12–25% of materials cost is being incurred without being recovered from the customer. For a business spending $350,000 per year on materials, that unbilled percentage represents $42,000–$87,500 per year in permanent margin destruction.

The second loss is invoice delay — the gap between completing a job and delivering the invoice. Every day of delay is a day of financing cost on the outstanding balance, and it is also a day during which the customer's memory of the service degrades and the likelihood of a payment dispute increases. When the invoice arrives three days after the service, the customer has moved on. They may not remember all the work that was done, they may question materials they do not remember seeing used, and they are less motivated to pay promptly for a service that feels historical rather than recent. Billing on the day of the job — or in the case of field billing software, within 60 seconds of job completion — eliminates every one of these delay-related problems at the source.

The third loss is receivables aging — invoices that are never followed up systematically and quietly move from 30 days outstanding to 60 days to 90 days to uncollectable, not because the customer deliberately refused to pay, but because the manual follow-up process that the business relied on was too inconsistent to catch every overdue balance. Automated payment reminders eliminate the consistency problem — every overdue invoice is followed up on schedule, without exception, without the business owner remembering to do it.

Time-and-Material Billing: GPS Hours and Work Order Materials

Time-and-material billing is the dominant billing method for field service businesses performing repair, troubleshooting, and maintenance work where the scope cannot be fully determined before the work begins. The customer is charged for actual hours worked at an agreed labour rate plus actual materials used at cost plus markup. The accuracy and credibility of a T&M invoice depends entirely on the quality of the underlying field data — if the hours are recorded inaccurately or the materials list is incomplete, the invoice is either inaccurate (undercharging) or disputeable (overcharging from the customer's perspective).

FieldZenPro's T&M billing generates invoices from two objective field data sources rather than from technician-reported data. Labour hours are calculated from GPS geofence events — the system records the timestamp when the technician enters the geofenced radius around the job site address, and the timestamp when they depart. The difference is the on-site duration applied at the configured labour rate. No timesheet entry. No rounding. No "I think it was about three hours" approximation from memory. The GPS record is the labour record — accurate to the minute, timestamped, and defensible in any dispute. Materials are logged in the digital work order during the job — the technician searches the materials catalogue on their mobile app and logs each item used with quantity. Every item logged flows to the invoice at the configured markup. No materials missed because they were too minor to write down. The T&M invoice is a complete record of every billable element of the job.

Flat-Rate Billing: Consistent Prices, Protected Margins

Flat-rate billing charges a fixed price for a defined scope of service, regardless of how long the job takes. The flat-rate price is set at a level that covers expected material cost, expected labour time for an efficient technician, overhead allocation, and target profit margin. When the job is completed faster than the time assumption, the business earns above-target margin. When complications extend the job, the business absorbs the additional time — a commercial risk that flat-rate pricing places on the contractor in exchange for the sales benefits of price certainty for customers.

FieldZenPro's flat-rate price book stores prices for standard field service tasks — organised by service category and accessible by the technician on their mobile app during the job. Prices are set centrally by the owner or operations manager and are immediately visible to all technicians when updated — price increases for a specific service category take effect across all technicians simultaneously, without the transition period of rolling out new paper price lists. The price book is the single source of truth for all customer-facing pricing. Pricing variation between technicians on the same service type — a problem that creates customer confusion when two customers compare prices for the same work — is eliminated because all technicians quote from the same price book.

Recurring Service Contract Billing: Predictable Revenue

Service contract billing generates invoices automatically at each billing interval for customers under ongoing service agreements — monthly maintenance contracts, quarterly inspection contracts, annual service agreements. The contract terms stored in FieldZenPro (billing amount, billing frequency, payment method, contract dates) drive the billing cycle without any manual invoice creation. When the billing interval arrives, the invoice is generated and delivered automatically. If the customer has authorised automatic payment, the charge is applied to their stored payment method immediately. If the customer pays manually, the invoice arrives with a payment link for convenient settlement.

The operational value of automated contract billing is not just time savings — it is consistency. Manual contract billing relies on the office administrator remembering to generate each contract invoice at the correct interval. In a busy field service operation, this manual process consistently produces billing gaps — contracts that should have been billed monthly but were only billed every 5–6 weeks, representing 1–2 months of annual revenue loss on each affected contract. Automated billing eliminates billing gaps entirely: every contract is billed on schedule, every billing period, without exception, without anyone having to remember to do it.

Progress Billing for Large Commercial Field Service Projects

Progress billing is the appropriate billing method for large field service projects — commercial HVAC installations, large electrical infrastructure projects, complex plumbing renovations — where the total project duration extends over multiple weeks and the contractor's cost accumulates continuously before project completion. Billing the entire project at completion would require the contractor to finance weeks of labour and materials from working capital — a cash flow strain that limits the number of large projects the business can take on simultaneously. Progress billing collects payment at defined milestones throughout the project, aligning cash inflows with cost outflows and reducing the working capital requirement.

FieldZenPro's progress billing module allows large projects to be structured as a schedule of values — a list of project phases with their percentage of total contract value. When a phase is completed, the project manager marks it complete in the system and the corresponding invoice generates automatically at the phase value, less any retainage percentage held by the customer. Retainage is tracked automatically across all milestone invoices — the running retainage balance is visible on the project summary dashboard and syncs to QuickBooks with the correct deferred revenue accounting. At project completion and final acceptance, the retainage release invoice is generated automatically totalling all withheld retainage from all milestone invoices.
