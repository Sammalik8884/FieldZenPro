Flat-Rate Price Books for Appliance Repair

The appliance repair flat-rate price book in FieldZenPro is organised by appliance category and failure type — allowing the technician to quickly find the correct flat price for any standard repair during or after diagnosis. The price book is searchable by keyword ("washing machine pump", "refrigerator compressor", "dryer heating element") and browsable by appliance category. Each price book entry includes the standard repair description, the flat customer-facing price, the estimated parts cost, and the estimated labour time that the flat rate is based on.

Flat-rate pricing serves the appliance repair business in three ways. First, customer trust: the customer knows the price before approving the repair, eliminating the anxiety of open-ended hourly billing on a complex appliance job. Second, margin consistency: every technician charges the same price for the same repair, regardless of their individual labour pace — margin is determined by efficiency, not by individual technician pricing judgment. Third, efficiency incentive: a technician who completes a flat-rate refrigerant recharge or compressor replacement in less than the estimated time earns above-target margin for the business on that job — the flat rate rewards the most skilled and experienced technicians proportionally to their output speed.

Warranty Job Management and Billing

Warranty jobs are a significant portion of many appliance repair businesses' volume — manufacturer-authorised service for appliances within the manufacturer warranty period, billed at the manufacturer's flat labour rates rather than the business's standard prices. Managing warranty jobs requires capturing specific information at booking (model number, serial number, purchase date, warranty reference number), billing at the manufacturer's prescribed labour rate for each repair category, and generating warranty claim documentation in the format required by each manufacturer's service portal.

FieldZenPro differentiates warranty jobs from standard jobs at the point of booking — the booking agent selects "warranty job" and enters the appliance's warranty details. The work order captures all required warranty claim fields during the repair. At completion, the billing applies the manufacturer's labour rate schedule for the repair category performed, and the warranty claim PDF is generated from the work order data. The technician submits the claim directly from the mobile app to the manufacturer's portal or emails the PDF to the warranty claims department. The warranty claim reference number is stored against the job for payment tracking.

Parts Ordering and Van Stock Management

Parts availability is the most common cause of non-billable return visits in appliance repair. When the technician arrives at a job with the diagnosed failure mode but without the correct part, one of three outcomes occurs: the technician orders the part and schedules a return visit (non-billable second trip), the technician attempts a temporary repair (risk of call-back), or the customer waits for the part to arrive before any work is performed (customer satisfaction impact). All three outcomes are worse than arriving with the part already available.

FieldZenPro's parts management reduces non-billable return visits in two ways. First, van stock visibility — the technician's van stock inventory is visible in the mobile app, allowing the dispatcher to confirm that the technician assigned to a booked repair has the likely parts for the expected failure mode before dispatching. Second, special-order part pre-ordering — when a part is not in van stock and the failure mode is known at the time of booking (customer describes symptoms that make the failure mode predictable), the parts request is created before the diagnostic visit and the part is on order while the technician schedules the appointment. The technician performs the diagnostic visit, confirms the failure mode and part, and the repair visit is scheduled for when the part arrives — a two-visit model that eliminates the "I'll have to order that part" conversation at the diagnostic visit.
